# Analysis of Procedure
When writing this procedure, I followed Redish’s principle of read-to-learn-to-do, writing for an audience focused on immediate, actionable tasks. I wrote for New NC State students, which means I looked to include basic explanations that discussed school portals and campus resources, while still aiming to keep it minimal and goal-oriented. Each step supports the user’s goal: to identify and report phishing email attempts. I wrote in a simple manner, attempting to stick to only the most relevant information to ensure the document remains practical and task-oriented. 
We spent a lot of time discussing that simplicity is most well-received in “learn to do” environments, so following Carroll’s minimalism, I limited instruction to what is needed in the moment of action. I kept the step names simple and comprehensive, and included very minimal secondary information or explanation. Because I am writing for new students, I chose not to eliminate explanation entirely, as in order to learn to do, students need to be aware of the resources first. In the spirit of minimalism, I only included two high-value tasks, with the goal of keeping each under 5 steps (and I succeeded, with both at only 4). 
The procedure itself follows Van Der Meij and Gellevij’s style guidelines; tasks are clearly separated, steps begin with verbs, and visuals are tied to the step they are for by immediately following the text they are about. Captions and figure numbers are also present to help the user navigate this document. I also maintained uniform, simplistic formatting with predictable sequencing. 
Images follow Arnheim’s principles for effective visualisation, with screenshots placed in a manner meant to reduce the cognitive load of steps by showing users exactly where and what to look for. I used cropping and highlighting as per the instructions to emphasise the points of interest, improving recognition and reducing any ambiguity within the steps. The figures are also labelled to further clarify which task or step they are associated with.
This procedure is designed to be instantly usable by a reader, visually clear and cohesive, and cognitively efficient for new NCSU students already overwhelmed with emails and information who need to learn or refresh their essential cybersecurity knowledge.
